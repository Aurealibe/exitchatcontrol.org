## Financial sovereignty

Money is surveilled and censorable too. Activists' accounts have been frozen, donations blocked, and every card payment is tracked. Financial privacy is part of sovereignty.

<ToolCard tool="t-bitcoin">
  <Fragment slot="name">Bitcoin (self-custody)</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="tags">
    <span class="tool-tag">🟡🔴</span>
    <span class="tool-tag">Censorship-resistant</span>
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="what">A digital money you hold yourself, without going through a bank.</Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="why">
    In <b>self-custody</b> (your keys in a wallet only you control), no one can freeze or block your
    funds. Its privacy isn't perfect (the ledger is public), but it escapes banking censorship,
    useful the day a payment is refused.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="who">
    Guarding against account freezes or de-banking, and paying anonymously for a service like a VPN.
  </Fragment>
  <ol slot="install">
    <li>Get some bitcoin (an exchange, or peer-to-peer).</li>
    <li>
      Move them right away to a wallet <b>you</b> control: a hardware wallet (Trezor, ColdCard) or
      an open-source app. Don't leave them on the exchange.
    </li>
    <li>Back up your recovery phrase on paper, never as a photo or online.</li>
  </ol>
</ToolCard>

<ToolCard tool="t-monero">
  <Fragment slot="tags">
    <span class="tool-tag">🔴 Advanced</span>
    <span class="tool-tag">Private by default</span>
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="what">
    The privacy cryptocurrency: amounts, sender and recipient are hidden by default.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="why">
    It's the exact opposite of Bitcoin here: transactions are private by design. It's the best way
    to pay for a service without leaving a trail back to you.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="who">
    Those who want maximum financial privacy. Note: Monero is already delisted from several European
    exchanges, so you often need a decentralised swap.
  </Fragment>
  <ol slot="install">
    <li>Install a wallet (the official one at getmonero.org, or Cake Wallet on mobile).</li>
    <li>
      Get XMR through a decentralised exchange service (for example a "swap" from another crypto).
    </li>
    <li>Back up your recovery phrase on paper.</li>
  </ol>
</ToolCard>

<ToolCard tool="t-wasabi">
  <Fragment slot="tags">
    <span class="tool-tag">🔴 Advanced</span>
    <span class="tool-tag">CoinJoin</span>
    <span class="tool-tag">🌍 · open-source</span>
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="what">
    A free Bitcoin wallet with built-in <b>CoinJoin</b>: your coins are combined with other users'
    coins in a joint transaction, which breaks the visible on-chain link between origin and
    destination.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="why">
    The Bitcoin ledger is public: without care, every payment can be linked to earlier ones.
    CoinJoin restores part of that privacy without handing your funds to anyone, and the wallet runs
    over Tor.
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="who">
    Experienced Bitcoin users. Since the original coordinator shut down in June 2024, you must pick
    an independent coordinator, which charges fees.
  </Fragment>
  <div class="box warn" slot="extra">
    <span class="lab">Mixed funds and platforms</span>
    <p>
      Regulated platforms may refuse or freeze funds that went through a CoinJoin. Keep a record of
      the legitimate origin of your funds.
    </p>
  </div>
  <ol slot="install">
    <li>
      Download Wasabi from <b>wasabiwallet.io</b> and verify the signature.
    </li>
    <li>In the settings, choose an independent coordinator.</li>
    <li>Turn on automatic CoinJoin, and never mix mixed and unmixed coins in the same payment.</li>
  </ol>
</ToolCard>

<div class="box honest">
  <span class="lab">On-chain privacy: what the law says</span>
  <ul>
    <li>
      <b>Swaps without an intermediary</b>: since August 2026, THORChain lets you swap bitcoin
      directly for Monero, with no account or platform. The service is new: a wrong return address
      can lose the funds.
    </li>
    <li>
      <b>Tornado Cash</b> (Ethereum): removed from US sanctions in March 2025, but its developers
      were prosecuted, with a money-laundering conviction in the Netherlands in 2024 (appeal
      pending) and a partial conviction in the United States in August 2025. The legal status of
      mixers remains uncertain in Europe.
    </li>
    <li>
      <b>July 2027</b>: the EU anti-money-laundering regulation (AMLR) bars regulated platforms from
      keeping anonymous accounts and from offering privacy-enhancing cryptocurrencies such as
      Monero. Holding in self-custody is not banned.
    </li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="box warn">
  <span class="lab">Legality &amp; caution</span>
  <p>
    Financial privacy is <strong>legal</strong>. Don't confuse it with tax evasion: declare what
    must be declared. Cryptocurrencies are <strong>volatile</strong> and scams are common; never
    risk what you can't lose, and learn before you act.
  </p>
</div>
